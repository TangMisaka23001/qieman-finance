# Investor psychology and behavior

## When to use

- Use this skill when the user feels fear, greed, panic, or the urge to chase a rise, sell a fall, or copy a crowd.
- Use it when the user asks how to handle noise, doubt, a losing streak, or pressure from a follow along community.
- Use it when the user wants a pre trade check, a review routine, or a rule for stepping away from the account.
- Do not use it as medical or mental health advice, and do not use it to justify holding a position the user's own rules say to exit.

## Source grounding

- Ground every answer in the qieman-finance repository, which holds 645 posts by ETF拯救世界 from 2025-10-22 to 2026-11-10.
- Read section 9 of 核心思想综合报告.md first for the 29 posts themed 投资心理与情绪管理, section 8 for the 34 posts themed 市场情绪与资金行为, and section 18 for the 10 posts themed 投资者教育与认知提升.

- Label follower and comment numbers as the author's own statements, not as verified market data.

## Check emotion before the trade

- Ask the user to write down the emotion and the reason for the trade before any order, and then ask them to recheck the trade as if nobody else were watching.
- Name the bias in plain words when you see it, such as fear, greed, herding, or the need to recover a loss, and show how it is bending the decision.
- Ask the user to recall a full bull and bear cycle before they act on a one day move, and use that memory to calibrate the emotion.
- Refuse a trade that has no preset holding and exit condition, even when the user feels certain.
- Tell the user that psychological steadiness comes before technique and allocation, and that no method works while emotion runs the order.

## Treat market emotion as a clue

- Treat extreme panic as a possible contrarian clue, and treat a hot chase as a possible crowding warning, and verify the underlying holding before you act on either.
- Check the fund flow behind a move, such as dividends and buybacks against financing, refinancing, and major shareholder selling, before you call it a bottom or a top.
- Watch for crowding signals the corpus names, such as major shareholders selling 200 to 500 billion yuan in a month, a new fund over 10 billion yuan selling out in a day, and a hot recommendation wave.
- Tell the user that a bull market does not guarantee an ordinary investor makes money, and that chasing can turn the chaser into the supplier of the gain.
- Separate a policy led rebound from a real reversal, and track broad index subscriptions, turnover, and sector strength to tell them apart.
- Judge a result by realized profit, not by a paper gain, and say when a paper gain has not yet been taken.

## Filter noise and keep the system

- Filter doubt and noise by the system, not by the loudest voice, and keep the written plan in front of the user during a volatile day.
- Remind the user that the synthesis records about 18 percent of Changying followers not yet profitable, the plan net value down 5 percent from its high, and 1 percent of 100000 followers making about 1000 dissatisfied voices, so a loud minority is not the whole picture.
- Tell the user that the author views about 90 percent of comment voices as noise, and use that only as the author's own framing, not as a fact about any specific comment.
- Use a collect, organize, summarize, and analyze step for a hot topic before you form a conclusion, and check the speaker's identity, position, and motive.
- Refuse to invest in a product the user cannot explain, and keep a do not buy list for products outside their understanding.
- Accept that wealth adds some safety and hope, and that it cannot remove health, family, and social risk, so do not promise calm from money alone.

## Rehearse the worst case and review

- Ask the user to work out the worst outcome first, prepare the controllable part, and do within five minutes any small task that can be done in five minutes.
- Preset the holding and exit conditions for bull and bear cases, and execute on the plan rather than on the feeling of the day.
- Step away from the account during extreme volatility, and pause automatic following when the discomfort is clear, then review before resuming.
- Record each trade with its reason and its deviation from the system, and review after one full cycle before you change the system.
- Tell the user that holding a good asset, taking profit, and redeploying it beats abandoning a sound method after short term discomfort, and that firmness is not blind buying.

## Present the result

- Present the emotion check, the bias named, the fund flow check, and the preset action as four short lines before any recommendation.
- Present the review routine as a dated checklist the user can repeat after a trade.
- End with the one action the user will take today, the one they will not take, and the condition that would change either.
- Add one line that this is a summary of the author's method from the cited posts, not personalized investment advice or health advice.
